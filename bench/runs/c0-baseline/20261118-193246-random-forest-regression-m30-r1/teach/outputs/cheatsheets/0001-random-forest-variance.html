Cheat sheet: why averaging trees works

From Lesson 1.

Prediction

Forest prediction = average of the trees' predictions. Each tree predicts the mean of the training targets in its leaf.

Variance of the average of B trees

Var = rho*sigma^2 + (1 - rho)*sigma^2 / B
  • sigma^2: variance of one tree's prediction; rho: correlation between two trees.
  • The second term shrinks with B. The first term, rho*sigma^2, is the floor.
  • rho = 1: no gain. rho = 0: sigma^2 / B.

Making rho small

  • Bagging: each tree gets a bootstrap sample of the rows.
  • Random forest: also a random subset of features at each split (max_features).

Limits

  • Averaging lowers variance, not bias shared by all trees.
  • No extrapolation: predictions stay within the range of training targets.
  • More trees past the floor do not help, and do not cause overfitting.
